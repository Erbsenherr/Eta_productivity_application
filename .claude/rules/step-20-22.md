### Step 20: a finished task gives its time back

Ticking a task off in "Heute anstehend" **takes it out of the day plan**. The stretch it
held is shaded green in the planner, anything may be planned into it, and leaving it
empty costs nothing.

**Two questions, not one.** `occupiesTime()` used to answer both "did the day carry
these minutes" and "is this block in the way", and those come apart here:

- **`occupiesTime()`** — the **accounting** question, which the settlement asks. A
  completed block carried its hours; a cancellation gave them back. Unchanged.
- **`claimsItsSlot()`** — the **placement** question, and narrower:
  `occupiesTime() && !block.isCompleted`. A task that is over is in nobody's way.

Everything that asks where something can go moved to the second: `standing()` and with
it `canPlace`, `firstFreeStart` and every drop in the planner, the dashboard's
`setStart` and `uncancel`, `conflictsOf`, `placeDynamicGrowth`, and the obstacles a
calendar import is compared against. The settlement asks the first and needed no change
at all.

**That is what makes it free.** `heldItsTime()` already counted a completed block, and
`plannedMinutes` merges spans — so the freed stretch is still covered whether or not
anything is planned into it. The user asked for exactly that: *tut er es nicht, soll ihm
das allerdings auch nicht zu Lasten fallen.* The guarantee is one line of the
settlement, and `FreedSlotTest` pins it from both sides. It follows, and was not
separately decided, that **planning something into the freed stretch and abandoning it
is also free**, the hours having already been earned. Outside the freed stretch the rule
from step 10 is untouched.

**The whole container comes back**, confirmed with the user: journey, task, journey back
and break together, because the stretch the block *occupied* is the stretch that is
freed. Someone who finishes at 14:00 gets the break they had planned for afterwards as
usable time, which is the point of the feature rather than a side effect.

**What the planner draws.** `PlannerUiState` carries `blocks` and `freed` separately, and
they are drawn differently on purpose: a cancellation is **not drawn at all** — its hours
left the plan and there is nothing to say about them — while a finished stretch is
shaded. `FreedStretch` is a green wash (`colors.successSoft`) and **carries the name**:
the reason for shading rather than leaving a plain gap is to say *why* the time is free,
and "you did that already" is the part worth reading. Drawn behind everything else and
without gestures — it is a record, not a card, and the moment something is planned into
it that something is what the eye should find. The place to take a tick back is the list
it was ticked off in. `hasFreeTime` had to move to the accounted set rather than the
standing one: an hour of free time already taken is still free time the day provided
for.

**Taking the tick back always works**, even when the freed hours have since been given
to something else. The block returns to its own time, the two overlap, and the
Überschneidung box says so — a mistyped tick has to be correctable. That is deliberately
the opposite of un-cancelling, which refuses: un-cancelling is a claim on a slot, while
this is undoing a statement about the past. No code was needed for it —
`toggleCompleted` already just reopens — which is a good sign that the two predicates are
cut in the right place.

### Step 21: finishing a card early, and where deleting belongs

Tapping a row on the Listen tab used to offer **Bearbeiten** and **Löschen**, and there
was no way to say a card was done without waiting for it to be planned into a day. The
popup now offers **Bearbeiten** and **Erledigt**, with deleting in an `EtaTrashButton`
in the top right corner, drawn in `colors.danger`. Both consequences ask first.

**An early completion is a block, because the Erfolgsliste is blocks.** There is no way
into that list except by giving a card a completed occurrence, so
`SmartListsViewModel.completeEarly` makes one — on **today**, starting **now**, with the
item's own estimated duration.

- A card that has never been concretized is **asked for category and duration** before it
  can be ticked off — see *Step 22*.
- A card that **already stands on today** is checked off where it stands, hour and
  margins intact, rather than being given a second occurrence — which the unique index
  would refuse anyway. One that was called off has its cancellation withdrawn in the
  same write: a block cannot be both, and "it is done" is the later statement.
- The start is pulled back far enough that the block still **ends inside the day**, or a
  two-hour task ticked off at 23:30 would ask the timeline to draw past midnight.
- `BlockOrigin.DRAGGED`, the same origin a card dropped onto the planner gets: the user
  put it there, on the day, by hand.
- `retireCompletedTodos` runs straight after — what the settlement would have done in the
  evening — so the ToDo reaches `Stage.DONE` and leaves the active lists at once rather
  than lingering in the Sammelliste it was just finished from.

**No points are credited here**, and the confirmation says so: the new block is harvested
with the rest of the day, or by `harvestLate` when that day is already settled. The hours
it covers therefore count as planned time that was held, which is right — the work
happened. Being completed it claims no slot, so it neither blocks a placement nor shows
up in the Überschneidung box.

**Not offered for a recurring card.** A standing task is never retired, and checking one
off would take every occurrence still to come with it. A bare recurring note therefore
keeps only Bearbeiten and the wastebasket; ending a standing task is what the
Wiederkehrend list is for.

The wastebasket is in the corner rather than in the button row because deleting is not
one of the two things a card is opened to do, and a thumb aiming at "Bearbeiten" should
not be able to land on it. **Both questions are put by the screen, not inside the
popup**: the popup closes and a `ConfirmDialog` opens in its place, since a dialog opened
from inside a dialog is a window on top of a window, and what is being confirmed is
precisely that the popup's card is about to leave the list. The inline "Wirklich?" is
gone with it; `RecurringGroupDialog` still uses that shape for "Beenden", which is right
there, that dialog having no second consequence to keep apart from.

### Step 22: what an early completion is worth, deadlines, and the block underneath

No schema change at all — the database stays at **version 17**, which is the interesting
part of the middle item.

**An early completion has to be priced.** Step 21 left the yield to whatever the card
happened to carry, and for a Quick-Add note that is nothing: no category, no length, and
those two *are* the yield. `CompleteItemDialog` now asks for both, **and only when they
are owed** — a card that is already `isConcretized` is simply confirmed. The figure is
shown while the answers are being given, because it is the consequence of them, and
`yieldOf(category, duration)` was pulled out of `yieldOf(item, block)` for it so the
number on the screen and the number the evening books are the same arithmetic rather
than two copies. The answers are written back to the **item** before the block is made,
because that is where `yieldOf` reads the category from. The priority is deliberately
left alone: a priority orders what is still to be planned, and this card is finished.

**`ReevaluationService.harvestLate` closes the one hole.** A card finished *after* today
was settled would earn nothing at all and say nothing about why, so that one case is
booked at once, from inside `ReevaluationService`, which keeps its rule literally true:
HARVEST is written in one place. The row carries a "nachgetragen" note, because a ledger
read a month later has to be able to tell a day's harvest from something added to it
afterwards. The dialog says which of the two will happen before it happens.

**A deadline is not a kind of card.** The user could not find the option to give a task a
deadline, and they were right: `ItemType.DEADLINE` and `Item.newDeadline` existed from
the first schema and **nothing ever called them**. The mistake was in the model — a
deadline is not a kind of card, it is something a card *has*. So `deadlineAt` moved into
`ItemExtras`, writing to the column `items` has carried since day one; **no migration**,
and none was ever needed.

- **Asked in the Extras box**, under the reminder, as a day stepper plus a clock — the
  Erinnerungen tab's shape, and for its reason: there is no date picker in this project,
  and a deadline a month out would otherwise be thirty taps away. The stepper stops at
  **today**: a deadline in the past is not a deadline, it is a missed one.
- **On ToDos only.** `ExtrasBox` takes a nullable `today`, the same gating idiom
  `onGrowth` uses, and the recurring and appointment forms pass none. A standing task is
  never "done by" a date, it simply goes on; an appointment has already been told the day
  and hour it stands on.
- **`observeOpenDeadlines` stopped asking for `type = 'DEADLINE'`** and now asks for a
  `deadlineAt`, an open card, and a stage that is neither `DONE` nor `LOCKED` — neither of
  those is something the user can still act on. Any old DEADLINE row qualifies unchanged.
- The box moved and is drawn only when it has something to say — see *The Heute tab*.

**Known limit:** a ToDo already planned into a day has no editable attributes on the
Listen tab, Tages- and Morgenliste being read-only, so its deadline cannot be changed
from there either. `BlockEditDialog` is where that would belong if it turns out to
matter.

**The block under the block.** Blocks are drawn full width at their own minute, so two
sharing an hour are stacked and only the last one drawn could receive a gesture — the one
underneath could not be long-pressed at all: not moved, not shortened, not called off.
The press now reports **the minute it landed on**, not just which block caught it, and
`coveringMinute` answers what else is drawn over that minute; more than one, and
`BlockChoiceDialog` asks which was meant.

- **The minute, not the block.** The press offset inside the block plus the block's own
  start is a minute of the day, clamped into the block so its last pixel still names a
  minute it covers. Everything else falls out of that.
- **The task's span, not the container.** The frame around a journey is drawn behind and
  carries no gestures, so what is under the finger is the task's own box and nothing else.
- **Only when there is really more than one.** A single block opens straight away: a
  dialog between every long press and every edit would be a tax on the ordinary case.
- **Held as ids, not rows**, and resolved against the current state each time it is drawn.
  A candidate that has gone while the dialog stood open leaves nothing to choose, and the
  dialog closes itself.

It answers the note's "gänzlich überschneiden" and the partial case with one rule,
because there is only one question: what is under the finger.
